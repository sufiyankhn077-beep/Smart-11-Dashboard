What is the Smart XI System?

Smart XI is a retrospective, context-aware player evaluation framework built on 200,999 ball-by-ball deliveries across 867 T20 & T20I matches (141 competitions). Rather than relying on raw averages or strike rates, every delivery is benchmarked against expected performance for the exact match format, innings, phase, wicket state, and pressure condition. Batters are ranked using an integrated score combining 70% Original Pressure Performance and 30% Scenario Impact (par-deviation, chase win-probability, run-rate lift, and difficult-entry stints). Bowlers are ranked using an integrated score combining 70% Original Pressure Performance and 30% Surge Scenario Impact (containment and wickets when opposition scoring surges ≥10 RPO or chase RRR ≥10).

Interpretation of Sensitivity & Bootstrap Uncertainty

Across 36 batter and 36 bowler sensitivity configurations (varying minimum ball thresholds, match minimums, and component weightings), the core Top 10 cohorts remain highly consistent. Match-cluster bootstrap confidence intervals (2,000 resamples for batters; 1,000 resamples for bowlers) quantify sampling variability across matches. These intervals are exploratory diagnostics computed with fixed context benchmarks and are not adjusted for multiple comparisons.

Match Context & Methodology

Pressure definitions, batter & bowler model architectures, context benchmarking hierarchy, and disclosed limitations.

Pre-Delivery Pressure Definitions & Distribution
200,961 innings 1–2 deliveries · 80,648 pressure deliveries (40.13%)
  • Collapse: 5 or more wickets already fallen before the delivery.
  • Late Innings: 30 or fewer scheduled legal balls remaining (>0).
  • Chase Deficit: 2nd innings, >12 legal balls remaining, required run rate exceeds current run rate by ≥2.0 RPO, runs still required.
  • Chase Wicket Danger: 2nd innings, >12 legal balls remaining, ≤4 wickets in hand and RRR ≥ CRR, runs still required.

Phases & Wicket Buckets

Phases: Powerplay (overs 1–6), Middle (7–15), Death (16–20). Wicket buckets: 0–2, 3–5, 6–9 wickets lost.

Original Pressure Components (70%): Adjusted pressure run output (35%), pressure vs. normal improvement (25%), survival/dismissal rate (15%), dot avoidance (10%), match consistency (15%). Reliability prior: 200 balls & 8 matches shrunk towards 50.

Scenario Impact Components (30%): Context-adj. runs/100 (10%), 1st-innings par-dev Δ (15%), chase win-prob Δ (15%), team RR Δ (10%), positive state-change consistency (15%), difficult-entry adj. runs (10%), difficult-entry state Δ (10%), chase exit favoured (5%), pressure strike rotation (10%). State model CV: Chase AUC 0.888 (Brier 0.137 vs 0.249); 1st-inn MAE 13.05 runs (R² 0.889).

Eligibility: ≥60 legal pressure & normal balls, ≥5 matches each; prior 120 balls & 8 matches.

Surge Scenario Components (30%): Economy control (30%), dot-ball control (20%), boundary prevention (20%), wicket advantage (20%), match consistency (10%). Eligibility: ≥30 surge legal balls, ≥3 surge matches, ≥3 qualifying matches with ≥6 surge balls.

Disclosed Limitations (Retrospective & Descriptive — Not a Prospective Prediction Model)

  • Descriptive, Not Causal: Scores describe historical performance under recorded match states; they do not establish causal effect or serve as a prospective forecasting/prediction model.
  • Retrospective Benchmarks: Context benchmarks and state models are fit on the full historical pool (including later matches); prospective use requires chronological holdout validation.
  • Competition & Role Differences: Combines T20I and T20 competitions without explicit opposition-strength or league-quality adjustments.
  • Unequal Opportunity & Non-Random Exposure: Players face different roles and pressure frequencies by team tactics and batting/bowling order.
  • DLS & Shortened Matches: Par-score/target adjustments in rain-affected games introduce noise (missing par scores in 9 of 408 result reconciliations).
  • Small Samples & Heuristic Reliability: Shrinkage priors (200/120 balls, 8 matches towards 50) are heuristic regularizers rather than empirical Bayes estimates.
  • Exploratory Bootstrap Uncertainty: Match-cluster bootstrap intervals treat context benchmarks as fixed and are not adjusted for multiple comparisons.
  • Batter Scenario Attribution: Team win-probability and par-deviation shifts during a stint reflect shared partner/team state movements as well as individual batting.
  • Scenario Feature Reproduction: The raw batter scenario feature-generation script is not included in the workbook package; downstream tables reflect its exported outputs.
